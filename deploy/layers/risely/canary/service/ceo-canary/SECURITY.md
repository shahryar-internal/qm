# CEO canary security boundary

This service is inert deployment scaffolding. It is deliberately outside `plugins/`, so QM cannot auto-discover, register, or scale it during a normal deployment. Authorized Terraform applies created the zero-desired-count ECS service, dedicated deny-all task role, dedicated execution role, ECR repository, private Cloud Map address, and empty secret containers, then registered digest-pinned task definition `risely-qm-pilot-ceo-canary:2` and updated only the zero-count service pointer. No application task has been launched, no canary path has been activated, and no provider traffic has occurred.

The canary is not rendered as a discovered QM plugin. Its current dedicated Terraform task injects only `CANARY_DATABASE_URL`, `CANARY_INGRESS_SECRET`, and `DATABASE_CA_CERT` through the canary execution role. `DATABASE_CA_CERT` maps to the dedicated `CANARY_DATABASE_CA_CERT` secret container rather than the mutable shared trust-anchor container. The task role has an explicit deny-all inline policy and no secret-read permission. `CANARY_MIGRATION_DATABASE_URL`, the dormant route-scoped QM observer secret, and every evaluation-writer or provider credential are excluded from the task and both ECS roles.

`CANARY_INGRESS_SECRET` is independent of `CORE_SIGNING_SECRET`. The dormant QM observer additionally requires `CANARY_QM_SHADOW_INGRESS_SECRET`, `CANARY_QM_SHADOW_INGRESS_AUDIENCE`, and `CANARY_QM_SHADOW_INGRESS_KEY_ID` to differ from the run/action authority. Its credential is accepted only for `POST /internal/v1/qm-shadow/observations` and has a 16 KiB limit; the general run/action authority retains its 256 KiB limit. The HMAC contract binds version, issuer, audience, key id, method, exact path, timestamp, nonce, content type, content length, and SHA-256 digest. The header MAC is verified before the request body is read. Raw bytes are streamed through the body MAC and digest under the selected route limit, fatal UTF-8 decoding, JSON depth and node limits, duplicate-key rejection, an eight-request pre-authentication budget, and a 64-connection server limit. Authenticated nonces are claimed durably after the complete body verifies. Failed unauthenticated requests do not create audit rows.

No reviewed caller is configured to receive the ingress secret. `CANARY_DEPLOYMENT_PROFILE_REF` is the sole runtime deployment-profile selector and must equal `deployment-profile:risely:ceo:v1`; the immutable production registry then resolves the complete CEO authority binding. No legacy Docker principal or identity variable is consumed, and the fixed profile reference is not proof of an acting human. Slack and web approvals remain rejected until a separate identity bridge verifies the human and binds that identity to the proposal. All mutation endpoints and provider execution are hard-disabled at startup and in the service layer, and the service constructor rejects any attempted enablement switch. Read endpoints also require the dedicated HMAC contract. `/livez` is liveness-only; `/healthz` and `/readyz` coalesce database-backed schema readiness checks and never write audit data.

Runtime startup accepts only `CANARY_DATABASE_URL` for database access. The public store has private monotonic initialization state, a captured pool boundary, fixed deployment authority, and no public SQL callback or pool access. Initialization and health perform the complete fixed database, role topology, migration, catalog, ownership, trigger, namespace, dependency, TOAST, and ACL attestation. Every later operation takes the shared maintenance lock and performs a same-client immutable sentinel check for the database and runtime role, namespace, sole version-8 checksum, catalog-authority digest, exact deployment-profile pair and stored record, absence of raw evaluation INSERT privilege, and absence of permission to execute the evaluation persistence routine. It then takes a profile-and-entity advisory lock for the affected ingress, run, action, outbox, or delivery identity. A changed future pool target, role, schema, migration, catalog authority, profile, or privilege fails before business SQL, while independent entities are not serialized behind one profile-wide lock. Migration and retention hold the maintenance lock exclusively and run the full attestation before and after maintenance. Periodic health performs the full catalog attestation needed to detect privileged external drift; that drift is outside the DDL-free runtime role's threat authority and remains an operational activation blocker. The verifier rejects connection overrides, credential reuse, missing CA material, role escalation, ownership or cross-schema authority, unexpected objects, unsafe routines or triggers, mutable immutable relations, catalog or ACL drift, extension or foreign-data authority, publication or subscription mappings, outside incoming foreign keys, and every uncompiled relation, column, sequence, routine, type, constraint, index, trigger, or TOAST tuple. Runtime, migration, retention, and exported verifiers pin `search_path=pg_catalog`; TLS uses the supplied CA and requires certificate verification.

Bootstrap rejects every enabled event trigger in database `qm` before its first role or schema DDL, and the centralized migration, runtime, and retention boundary repeats that database-wide check. Disabled event triggers, cluster-level configuration, other databases, and administrator activity remain infrastructure inventory rather than application authority. Activation therefore still requires the infrastructure administrator to attest that broader state.

The namespace boundary also rejects direct extension membership of the canary schema, subscription relations, schema-less casts or transforms involving canary types, ownership or effective `USAGE` of every foreign-data wrapper by any fixed role, effective foreign-server `USAGE`, and fixed-role or `PUBLIC` user mappings. Effective wrapper `USAGE` includes privileges inherited from `PUBLIC` even when no foreign server exists. These checks run in migration preflight, post-migration verification, runtime readiness, and both retention attestations where the relevant objects can exist.

The runtime and migrator are distinct login roles in the existing Risely QM database named `qm`; a third `NOLOGIN` role owns the canary schema and objects, and a fourth evaluation-writer role remains `NOLOGIN` until its separate workload boundary is approved. The fixed database, port, schema, role, and trusted bootstrap-administrator identities are server constants. Request payloads cannot redirect a connection. Both URLs must match the deployment-configured host, port 5432, and database `qm`, so a simulated Command Center or other foreign host is refused when the expected host is authentic. The managed Terraform task consumes `CANARY_DATABASE_HOST`, `CANARY_DATABASE_PORT`, and `CANARY_DATABASE_NAME` directly from the existing QM RDS resource and fixes `CANARY_DEPLOYMENT_PROFILE_REF` to `deployment-profile:risely:ceo:v1`. Independently observed task revision 2 matches the closed source seal, but activation remains blocked because the observation is evidence rather than authority and every live requirement remains unmet. Runtime receives only canary-schema DML privileges. Migrator has no inherited owner rights and may only set the fixed owner role; the owner cannot log in. Bootstrap is transactional and one-shot: it requires a direct `qm` non-superuser `LOGIN CREATEROLE` database-owner session, fails if the supplied psql bootstrap identity differs or any fixed role or schema exists, creates all four roles with `PASSWORD NULL` and `NOLOGIN`, and never adopts or rotates an existing object. PostgreSQL 16 automatically records an ADMIN-only creator edge from each created role to `qm`, granted by exact bootstrap-superuser role OID 10; PostgreSQL does not permit a non-superuser creator to remove those edges. This adds no new trust domain because `qm` already owns and administers the shared database. The exact accepted topology is those four automatic `ADMIN=true`, `SET=false`, `INHERIT=false` OID-10 edges plus one owner-to-migrator `SET=true`, `INHERIT=false`, `ADMIN=false` edge granted by `qm`. A temporary separate SET-only owner edge lets `qm` create the owner-authorized schema and is revoked before structural commit. Credential provisioning is a separate atomically re-runnable operator transaction. It opens secret paths with no-follow and nonblocking flags, accepts only bounded owner-only regular files, and validates two distinct generated credential encodings before opening psql. A FIFO, symlink, device, malformed file, or partial read fails promptly before any child or database phase. The provisioner uses only a protected `PGPASSFILE` for administrator authentication, sends both confirmations and `COMMIT` in one complete stdin stream, enables SCRAM logins, requires exact postflight topology and a success marker, clears buffers, and authenticates both roles over verify-full TLS. EOF, missing or malformed files, partial login state, topology drift, or failed post-authentication keeps activation blocked.

PostgreSQL normally grants database `TEMPORARY` through `PUBLIC`. This service truthfully permits only that standard inherited capability, rejects every direct canary-role `TEMPORARY` or `CREATE` grant, and never emits temporary-object SQL. This remains a shared-resource denial-of-service risk if a canary credential is compromised; removing it would require a separately reviewed shared-QM ACL change that this layer does not perform. Pool size is capped at eight, connection establishment at five seconds, statements at ten seconds for runtime and thirty seconds for operator migration/retention, and the server caps ingress connections. Those limits reduce but do not eliminate the inherited-temp risk. All canary roles must still have no effective database `CREATE`, non-canary schema ownership or `CREATE`, non-canary table or sequence access, or reachable non-system `SECURITY DEFINER` routine.

Candidate and judge rows bind the deployment-profile pair while storing distinct evaluation-profile and evaluation-policy digests; release rows bind the exact policy reference and digest, so none of those identities can be substituted for another. Mutable CEO-surface state is split into exact-column outbox-state and delivery-reservation relations. Nothing deployed the earlier CEO-named database design. The immutable migration checksum binds the migration preflight and body, catalog projection, verifier implementation, and every expected relation, column, sequence, routine, type, ACL, trigger, topology, access-method, collation, extension-ownership, and dependency tuple. The `NOLOGIN` owner can alter immutable relations and is assumed by the verified migrator only for explicit migration and retention. Migration and retention take the maintenance advisory lock exclusively. Every core canary transaction and every surface transaction takes that lock in shared mode before any canary relation or row lock, then takes a profile-and-entity advisory lock for the affected durable identity. This establishes one lock order, excludes retention from active state transitions, and permits independent entities to progress concurrently. Under the same retention transaction and maintenance lock, the complete post-migration catalog, topology, ACL, trigger, and dependency attestation runs immediately before deletion and again after deletion before commit. A foreign trigger, reachable `SECURITY DEFINER` routine, incoming foreign key, or other attested drift therefore prevents retention DML or rolls it back with no external deletion effect. Expiry atomically records a permanent event tombstone before committing the failed state. Retention deletes resolved terminal action state, events, and associated audit after 180 days, but deletes delivered, definitively failed, or expired CEO-surface live rows only when every stored conflict digest matches its permanent immutable tombstone. It never deletes either tombstone class. It deletes unreferenced runs after 365 days and eligible audit after 400 days. Recursive run-lineage protection retains every ancestor referenced by a retained child. Retention never deletes unresolved actions, an attempting or outcome-unknown CEO delivery, or effect reservations, so ambiguous provider outcomes remain available for reconciliation and completed effect, delivery, and expired event identities remain permanently reserved.
